## Module: Course Content (`main`)

### Lesson: California Renewal Overview (`renewal-overview`)

#### LEARNING OBJECTIVES
- Explain California CNA certification renewal hours and timelines.
- Describe the birthday rule and four-year record-keeping requirement.
- Identify how online CE hour caps affect course planning.

#### MAIN CONTENT

##### Why Renewal Rules Exist
California requires certified nursing assistants to complete continuing education (CE) and in-service training to keep skills current and protect public safety. The California Department of Public Health (CDPH) oversees CNA certification. If you let your certificate expire or fail to meet hour requirements, you may not legally work as a CNA until you are back in compliance—sometimes requiring re-testing or retraining.

This course gives practical guidance. **CDPH makes the final decision** on whether your documentation is acceptable. Always verify current rules on official CDPH sources when you renew.

##### Total Hours and Annual Minimum
California CNAs must complete **48 hours** of combined continuing education and in-service training during each **two-year certification period**. That equals **24 hours per year** on average, but CDPH also expects at least **12 hours in each individual year** of the two-year cycle. You cannot skip an entire year and cram all 48 hours into the second year.

Hours come from two buckets:
- **Continuing Education (CE)**—often outside courses like this platform
- **In-Service Training**—employer-provided training on the job

Both count toward the 48-hour total when properly documented. Know which type each class is when you log hours.

##### Online CE Cap (24-Hour Rule)
Of the 48 hours required per renewal period, **no more than 24 hours** may come from **online** CE courses. The remaining hours must come from in-person training, live interactive formats accepted by CDPH, or in-service hours per current policy. Plan early if you rely heavily on online learning so you do not hit the cap and still owe hours.

##### Birthday Rule (Certification Expiration)
Your CNA certificate is tied to your **birthday**. Certification generally expires on your birthday **two years** after initial certification or last renewal. Mark this date on your calendar. Start gathering certificates at least **90 days before** expiration to avoid gaps in employment.

If you renew late, employers may remove you from the schedule until CDPH confirms active status.

##### Four-Year Record Keeping
California requires CNAs to **keep proof of CE and in-service completion for four years**. Keep:
- Certificates of completion with your name, course title, date, hours, and provider information
- Employer in-service sign-in sheets or electronic records
- Any official transcripts or forms

Store copies digitally and physically. If CDPH audits or your employer verifies, missing records can cost you work time even if you actually attended classes.

##### Mandatory Topics
California expects CNAs to cover required topics such as **infection control** and **elder/dependent adult abuse** during renewal cycles. Track not only hours but subject matter. A random collection of classes without mandatory topics may not meet compliance expectations.

#### KEY POINTS
- 48 hours per two-year period; minimum 12 hours each year.
- Maximum 24 of 48 hours may be online CE—plan the rest as in-person/in-service.
- Certificate expires on your birthday every two years—renew early.
- Keep documentation four years; certificates must show hours and dates.
- Include mandatory topics like infection control and elder abuse education.

#### KNOWLEDGE CHECK

**Question 1:** A CNA completes 30 hours of online courses in year one and wants 18 more online hours in year two. Is this plan compliant?

**Answer:** No. Online CE is capped at 24 hours per renewal period. They need non-online/in-service hours for the remaining 24 hours of the 48-hour total, and must still meet 12 hours minimum in each year.

**Question 2:** Your birthday renewal is in 60 days and you have 40 hours documented. What should you do now?

**Answer:** Complete at least 8 more approved hours immediately, confirm mandatory topics are covered, verify online cap not exceeded, organize certificates for four-year storage, and prepare your CDPH 283A hour documentation and 283C renewal application for submission per instructions.

### Lesson: CDPH Forms & Documentation (`cdph-forms`)

#### LEARNING OBJECTIVES
- Describe purpose of CDPH 283A and 283C forms for CNA renewal.
- List documentation elements employers and CDPH expect on certificates.
- Organize a personal renewal file to reduce errors and delays.

#### MAIN CONTENT

##### CDPH Forms Overview
CDPH uses standardized forms for CNA certification actions. Two forms commonly discussed during renewal:

**CDPH 283A (In-Service Training / Continuing Education Units)**—Used to document completed CE/in-service hours. It is proof of hours, not the renewal application itself. Keep certificates and your 283A documentation for four years.

**CDPH 283C (Renewal Application)**—The application used to renew a CNA or HHA certificate, and also used for CNA reactivation when renewal requirements were not met and the certificate has been expired less than two years (Question 6). Submit the 283C to CDPH per current instructions (online portal or mail as applicable).

Forms change occasionally. Download the latest version from official California government sources. This course cannot submit forms for you.

##### Information You Typically Need
Before completing forms, gather:
- CNA certificate number
- Social Security number (for identity verification)
- Home address and contact information
- Employer information if employed
- List of CE/in-service hours with dates, providers, and topics
- Signatures and dates attesting truthfulness

False attestation can have professional and legal consequences. Only claim hours you personally completed.

##### Acceptable Proof of Hours
Each training record should show:
- **Your full legal name** matching certification
- **Course or in-service title**
- **Date of completion**
- **Number of hours** (or clear calculation from seat time if stated)
- **Provider name and contact** (school, employer, approved vendor)

If a certificate lacks hours, request a corrected certificate before renewal. Employers sometimes issue letters on letterhead summarizing in-service—keep originals.

##### Organizing Your Renewal Binder (Physical or Digital)
Create sections:
1. **Certification**—copy of CNA card, expiration date
2. **CE certificates**—labeled by year
3. **In-service records**—by employer and year
4. **Mandatory topics checklist**—infection control, elder abuse, etc.
5. **Submitted forms**—copy of 283A/283C and proof of delivery

Update after every class. Waiting until renewal week causes panic and gaps.

##### Employer Role vs. Your Role
Employers may help verify in-service hours but **you** are responsible for maintaining copies and submitting renewal to CDPH. HR delays do not extend CDPH deadlines automatically. Follow up politely but firmly.

##### Online Course Documentation From This Platform
When you complete courses here, download completion certificates immediately. Confirm displayed CE hours match catalog. Store PDFs in your renewal folder with course title and completion date.

#### KEY POINTS
- 283A documents completed CE/in-service hours; 283C is the renewal application (and the form used for CNA reactivation when eligible).
- Certificates must include name, title, date, hours, and provider.
- Organize records by year with mandatory topic tracking.
- You—not only your employer—must keep four years of proof and submit on time.
- False hour claims are serious; document only training you completed.

#### KNOWLEDGE CHECK

**Question 1:** A certificate shows your nickname, not legal name, and no hour count. Is it sufficient?

**Answer:** No. Request a corrected certificate with legal name and explicit hours before using it for CDPH renewal documentation.

**Question 2:** Certificate expired last month. You have all 48 hours. Can you use only 283A?

**Answer:** No. 283A documents your hours; the renewal or reactivation application is the CDPH 283C. If renewal requirements were not met and the certificate has been expired less than two years, you may be able to reactivate by taking the Competency Evaluation Examination—submit 283C with "Yes" on Question 6; CDPH decides eligibility and sends exam instructions. Check official guidance immediately; working with expired certification may be prohibited.
