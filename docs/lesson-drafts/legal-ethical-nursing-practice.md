## Module: Course Content (`main`)

### Lesson: Scope of Practice & Patient Rights (`scope-of-practice`)

#### LEARNING OBJECTIVES
- Define CNA scope of practice under California nursing delegation rules.
- Explain resident rights, HIPAA privacy, and professional boundaries.
- Document care objectively and escalate actions outside your certification.

#### MAIN CONTENT

##### CNA Scope of Practice in California
Certified nursing assistants provide nursing-related services under supervision of licensed nurses (RN or LVN) and within state-approved training. Your scope includes activities you were taught in an approved CNA program and that facility policy assigns—such as bathing, dressing, grooming, feeding, ambulation, transferring, measuring intake and output, recording vital signs when delegated, and observing resident condition.

**Inside scope:** Reporting changes, assisting with activities of daily living, using assistive devices per care plan, maintaining cleanliness, supporting restorative programs as directed.

**Outside scope:** Nursing assessment, diagnosing, prescribing medications, administering medications (except as allowed by very specific state/facility programs—most CNAs do not medicate), interpreting medical orders, designing care plans, performing sterile procedures not in training, and independent clinical decision-making.

When asked to do something outside scope, say calmly, "That is not within my CNA role. I will get the nurse." Never practice beyond certification to please coworkers or save time.

California Title 22 and nurse practice act regulations shape delegation. Only licensed nurses delegate nursing tasks. Follow written care plans and verbal orders within your training.

##### Resident Rights and Dignity
Federal OBRA regulations and California law protect nursing home residents' rights to dignity, privacy, participation in care, refusal of treatment (with limits), freedom from abuse and restraints, grievances without retaliation, and confidential treatment of personal and medical information.

Daily CNA actions either uphold or violate these rights:
- Knock and identify yourself before care
- Cover bodies during care; close doors
- Offer choices when safe
- Honor cultural and personal preferences when feasible
- Never use restraint, force, or humiliation for convenience

**Refusal of care:** Residents may refuse hygiene or food when alert and capable unless court-appointed decision-maker or medical incapacity processes apply. Notify nurse, document, and monitor health impact.

**DNR and advance directives:** POLST and DNR orders guide emergency response. Verify identifiers and orders per facility policy before emergencies—do not assume.

##### HIPAA and Confidentiality
Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) protects protected health information (PHI). CNAs may access PHI only for work. Do not discuss residents in public hallways, elevators, parking lots, or social media. Share information only with team members involved in care or persons the resident authorized.

Report lost charts, overheard PHI breaches, or snooping in electronic records per facility privacy officer policy.

##### Professional Boundaries
Boundaries protect residents from exploitation and staff from false accusations:
- No romantic or sexual relationships with residents—ever
- Avoid expensive gifts or borrowing money
- Do not share personal social media or home address
- Decline residents' requests that violate policy even if they seem friendly
- Treat all residents equally without favoritism

Boundary crossings start small ("just this once"). Stop early.

##### Documentation Standards
Document **objective** observations: what you saw, heard, measured, and did. Use quotes for resident statements when important. Include time and notification to nurse.

**Good:** "1430: Resident refused PM shower, stated 'too tired.' RN Martinez notified 1432. Skin warm, dry, intact."

**Poor:** "Resident lazy about shower."

Accurate documentation is legal protection for residents and you. Altering or falsifying records is a serious violation.

##### Ethics in Daily Practice
Ethics means doing right when rules are silent. Examples:
- Tell the truth on time sheets and incident reports
- Report abuse and unsafe staffing even when uncomfortable
- Admit mistakes immediately so residents get timely treatment
- Respect advance directives even when emotionally difficult

When ethical conflict arises (family demands vs. resident wish), involve nurse, social services, and ethics resources.

#### KEY POINTS
- CNA scope is hands-on care under nurse supervision—no diagnosing or medicating (unless specific authorized program).
- Resident rights and HIPAA apply every shift; refusals and privacy need nurse involvement.
- Professional boundaries prevent harm and license risk.
- Document objective facts with times and notifications.
- Report scope violations, abuse, and unsafe orders through proper channels.

#### KNOWLEDGE CHECK

**Question 1:** Physician tells CNA to adjust insulin dose based on sliding scale. Appropriate response?

**Answer:** Decline—insulin adjustment is outside CNA scope. Notify licensed nurse immediately to perform medication task.

**Question 2:** Resident offers you $200 for extra favors. What do you do?

**Answer:** Politely refuse, explain policy prohibits accepting money, notify supervisor/nurse, document interaction objectively, and maintain professional boundaries.

### Lesson: Final Exam (`final-exam`)

#### LEARNING OBJECTIVES
- Review legal and ethical themes for California CNAs.
- Apply scope, HIPAA, documentation, and resident rights to exam scenarios.
- Prepare for final assessment certificate eligibility.

#### MAIN CONTENT

##### Exam Content Areas
The final exam typically covers:
- **Scope of practice** vs. nursing/medical tasks
- **Resident rights** under federal and California expectations
- **Mandatory reporting** of abuse (overlap with elder abuse law)
- **HIPAA** privacy and permitted disclosures
- **Documentation** objectivity, timeliness, and legal significance
- **Professional boundaries** and social media risks
- **Advance directives, POLST, DNR** at CNA recognition level
- **Ethical principles:** autonomy, beneficence, nonmaleficence, justice—applied practically

You do not need legal jargon. Choose answers that protect resident safety, respect rights, follow policy, and involve licensed nurses for clinical decisions.

##### Scenario Strategies
**Scope questions:** If task requires assessment or prescription, CNA does not do it—notify RN/LVN.

**HIPAA questions:** No resident information in public or personal social media; verify identity before sharing information with callers per facility policy.

**Rights questions:** Support refusal when competent; investigate forced care scenarios as potential rights violation; know restraint requires physician order and strict criteria.

**Documentation questions:** Facts, times, quotes; no blaming language; report changes promptly.

**Ethics questions:** Truth-telling, reporting errors, refusing to falsify records, whistleblower protections for mandated abuse reports.

##### Advance Directives Review
**Advance directive** documents name healthcare decision-makers when resident cannot decide. **POLST** (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) translates goals into medical orders for emergencies. **DNR** means no CPR if breathing and heart stop—verify wrist identifiers and chart orders. CNAs initiate CPR unless valid DNR/POLST and policy clearly indicate otherwise—know facility emergency algorithm.

##### Social Media and Cell Phones
Photographing residents or posting workplace stories violates HIPAA and resident dignity even if faces hidden. Use facility-approved devices only per policy. Report violations you witness.

##### Professionalism Under Stress
Short staffing does not justify practicing out of scope or skipping documentation. Ethical practice includes advocating for safe assignments through chain of command.

##### Final Exam Preparation
Re-read scope of practice section, list what you must report (abuse, major errors, privacy breaches), and practice eliminating answer choices that involve diagnosing, medicating without authority, gossiping about PHI, or ignoring refusals.

Passing score is 80% for certificate eligibility. Take breaks if needed; read each question twice for "best" vs. "also true" options.

#### KEY POINTS
- Exam tests practical legal/ethical choices, not advanced law theory.
- When unsure, choose nurse involvement, resident rights, privacy, and objective documentation.
- DNR/POLST require verification before withholding CPR—follow facility algorithm.
- Social media and gifts are common boundary/exam traps.
- Mandatory abuse reporting is legal duty, not optional ethics.

#### KNOWLEDGE CHECK

**Question 1:** Family demands lab results by phone; you are unsure if resident authorized release. Action?

**Answer:** Follow facility HIPAA verification policy—do not release PHI until authorized verifier confirms. Offer to have nurse return call.

**Question 2:** You document a fall you caused by leaving bed alarm off against care plan. Ethical documentation?

**Answer:** Report truthfully in incident report and chart: alarm off, fall occurred, times, assessments, notifications. Falsifying or omitting facts endangers residents and constitutes fraud.
